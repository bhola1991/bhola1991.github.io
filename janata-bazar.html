A produce business, and the software holding it together

Fresh produce retail and wholesale across South Kolkata. I direct the operation, architected the three Google Play apps it runs on, and build the internal tooling directly. Sourcing from farmers, distributing through 19+ franchise outlets.

What I run

  • Operations across 19+ franchise outlets, procurement through to shelf
  • A zero-inventory-capital model — working capital stays free because stock never sits still
  • Wholesale spanning inter-market trading, bulk imports, sesame exports and HoReCa supply
  • Vendor ecosystems, financial modelling, and logistics lean enough to survive a bad monsoon
  • Content and brand across YouTube and Facebook, co-led with partners

What I built

  • Backend systems and workflow automations, built with Codex and Claude Code, in daily production use
  • The architecture and specification for three production Android apps — farmer, customer and delivery partner — built out by a development team and shipped to Google Play
  • Internal tools built directly with Codex and Claude Code, opened daily by non-technical staff, covering operations, content and franchise management
  • Distributed workflows adapted from blockchain architecture, to remove single points of failure in the supply chain
  • The full content pipeline: shoot coordination, editing, design, scheduling

Why this is unusual

Software built for an operating business fails in a specific way. It isn't that the code is wrong. It's that the person who wrote it has never had to use it at six in the morning with a truck waiting, and so it assumes a patience nobody has.

Building for a business I run myself removed that gap. Every tool here had to survive its own author using it under pressure, in front of staff who would simply stop opening it if it wasted their time. Several early versions died that way. What's left works because it had to.

The constraints that shaped it

  • Users who will not read documentation, ever, and shouldn't have to
  • Data arriving as photographs of handwritten ledgers, phone calls, and WhatsApp messages
  • Nineteen locations that each do things slightly differently and are all correct
  • No engineering team to escalate to when something breaks at 5am
